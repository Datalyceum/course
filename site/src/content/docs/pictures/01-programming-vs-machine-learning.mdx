## The puzzle

A motor in a factory. Temperature is normal. Vibration is under its limit. Current is under its limit. No alarm. Two days later, the motor fails.

Nothing was broken in the monitoring system. It did exactly what it was told. The problem is how it was told.

## Rules written by a person

Most plant monitoring starts with thresholds. An engineer writes the rules: if the temperature goes above 90 °C, raise an alarm. If vibration goes above its limit, call maintenance.

These rules are clear and easy to check, and they catch known problems well. But each rule looks at **one sensor at a time**. In our motor, every reading stayed under its own line, so no rule fired.

## Rules learned from data

Machine learning turns the job around. Instead of writing the rules, you give the computer two things:

- **Data:** years of sensor readings, such as temperature, vibration, current and pressure.
- **Answers:** the record of every past failure, with its date.

The computer looks for what came before each failure. Here it finds a combination no single rule could see: **vibration and current rising together**, while the temperature still looks normal. Those learned rules are called the **model**.

The model doesn't answer yes or no. It gives a probability, such as "78% risk of failure this week". That's enough to plan the repair before the motor breaks. This use of machine learning is called **predictive maintenance**.

## In one line

- **Programming:** rules + data → answers
- **Machine learning:** data + answers → rules (the model)

## Things to keep in mind

- **The alarms stay.** In real plants a model like this runs next to the threshold alarms, not instead of them. The thresholds still protect against limits you already know.
- **No failures, no learning.** The model can only learn warning signs that appear in its history. If a kind of failure never happened before, the model has never seen what comes before it.
- **The 78% is an example.** In this picture it shows what a risk score looks like, not a real measurement.
