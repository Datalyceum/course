# Lesson 1.2: quiz after the lesson

**1. A typo turns one penguin's 4,250 g into 42,500 g. What happens to the mean and the median body mass?**

- A) Both go up a lot
- B) The mean goes up, the median stays the same
- C) The median goes up, the mean stays the same

**2. For all penguins, the standard deviation of body mass is 802 g. Inside each species it's about 400 to 500 g. Why is it smaller inside a species?**

- A) The smaller groups have fewer penguins
- B) Each species has its own typical size, so mixing them adds spread
- C) The scale was more accurate for each species

**3. A histogram of flipper length has two humps. What is the most likely explanation?**

- A) Two groups with different typical values are mixed together
- B) The data has duplicates
- C) The mean is wrong

**4. The middle half of the body masses runs from 3,550 to 4,750 g. What does that tell you?**

- A) Half of the penguins weigh between 3,550 and 4,750 g
- B) The average penguin weighs 4,150 g
- C) No penguin weighs less than 3,550 g

<summary>Show answers</summary>

1. B. The mean adds up every value, so one huge typo moves it, from 4,202 to 4,313 g. The median only depends on the middle of the sorted list, so it stays at 4,050 g.
2. B. Gentoos are much heavier than Adelies and Chinstraps. Put them together and the values spread from the light group to the heavy one. Inside one species the values sit closer to their own middle.
3. A. Two humps usually mean two groups: here Adelie and Chinstrap penguins around 190 mm, and Gentoos around 215 mm.
4. A. The middle half runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile, so a quarter of the penguins are lighter and a quarter are heavier. It doesn't give the average.
