## Stretch (no code): describe flipper length

1. Open [penguins.csv](../../../data/penguins/penguins.csv) in any spreadsheet.
2. For the `flipper_length_mm` column, work out the mean, the median, the smallest and largest value, and the standard deviation. In Excel or Google Sheets: `AVERAGE`, `MEDIAN`, `MIN`, `MAX` and `STDEV.S`.
3. Do the same for each species on its own. Tip: sort by species, or use a filter.
4. Are the mean and median close? Is the spread for each species smaller or bigger than for all penguins together? Why?

Bonus: draw a histogram of flipper length (in Excel: Insert → Chart → Histogram). How many humps do you see, and which species make each one?

Post what you found in the [homework thread](L12THREAD).

## Challenge: your own data

Find a number column you care about, from your work, your city or a public data portal: prices, waiting times, salaries, sales. Run the routine from the lesson:

1. Count the values, and the gaps.
2. Find the mean and the median.
3. Measure the spread: the range, the middle half and the standard deviation.
4. Draw a histogram and look at the shape.
5. Split by a group, if your data has one.

Are the mean and median far apart? What pulls them apart? Which one would you put in a report, and why? Post your answers in the [homework thread](L12THREAD).

Want to do it in code? [describe-penguins.ipynb](describe-penguins.ipynb) runs every step on the penguin file with pandas.
